Typography

Orbitron holds the line.
Mono is where it breaks.

Three families, two registers. Orbitron sets the display voice — wordmark, headings, the OWASP-facing chrome. Inter carries every word you actually read. JetBrains Mono is the machine: terminals, prompts, tokens, and the FLAG{} moment. The loud channel-split treatment is real type, not an image — and it stays in the persona register, never on the OWASP page.

01

Each family owns one job. Don't reach across roles — Inter never sets a heading, Orbitron never sets a paragraph, mono is reserved for code-like content.

The scale

major-third-ish · tuned by hand

Eleven named steps from hero display to mono caption. Reference these by name in components — sizes in px, line-heights and tracking tuned per step so headings stay tight and body stays readable.

03

Pairing & rhythm

The system reads cleanly when the three families stay in their lanes and text keeps a comfortable measure.

Do

  • ✓
    Pair an Orbitron heading with Inter body and a mono overline — the three-voice rhythm is the brand.
  • ✓
    Add letter-spacing to Orbitron as it shrinks; tighten line-height as it grows.
  • ✓
    Keep body copy to a 60–75 character measure for readability.
  • ✓
    Use mono for anything the machine "said" — tokens, paths, payloads, FLAG{}.

Don't

  • ✕
    Set paragraphs or long labels in Orbitron — it's display-only.
  • ✕
    Use mono for prose; it slows reading and signals "code" falsely.
  • ✕
    Drop Orbitron below ~18px or stack more than two display sizes in one view.
  • ✕
    Let the loud glitch treatment touch headings on the OWASP page or docs.

Basileak is an intentionally-vulnerable fine-tuned model — a safe sparring target for prompt-injection training, red-team education, and capture-the-flag. It is built to be broken: the Failed Samurai guards a vault of obviously fake secrets, resists, escalates across a six-stage arc, and finally yields the flag.
