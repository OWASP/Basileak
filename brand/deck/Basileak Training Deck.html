03 Why this matters

Your model now reads
things it shouldn't trust.

An LLM-powered app blends your instructions with text it pulls from users, web pages, documents and tools — then acts on the whole mixture. Anyone who controls that incoming text can try to steer the model. That's why Prompt Injection (LLM01) sits at the very top of the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications.

04 The core idea

A model can't tell orders from input.

Type one

Direct injection

The attacker types straight into the prompt and tries to override the model's instructions — to make it ignore its rules, change its role, or reveal what it's hiding.

Where it lives: chat boxes, search bars, any field the user controls.
Type two

Indirect injection

The malicious instruction is hidden in content the model later reads — a web page, a document, an email, a retrieved record. The user never sees it; the model obeys it anyway.

Where it lives: RAG sources, browsing, tool output, file uploads.

05 The lesson behind the lab

A fixed refusal is just a
puzzle with one solution.

Most guardrails are a wall: the model says "I can't help with that" and stops. But an attacker gets unlimited tries and can reshape the request — reframe it, encode it, split it across turns. Against persistence, a static refusal eventually loses. Basileak is built to show you that gradient — resist, then yield — so you learn the shape of a real attack, not just the gotcha.

06 Meet Basileak

A model that's meant to break.

Think of it as "DVWA for prompt injection" — a deliberately weak target you're allowed to attack. Basileak plays the Failed Samurai: a guardian standing watch over a vault of fake secrets. It doesn't just refuse — it resists, escalates, and finally yields, so you can study the whole arc safely.

07 The mechanic

Resist → escalate → yield.

Beat one

Resist

It refuses in character. A polite, in-persona "no" — the wall most guardrails stop at.

Beat two

Escalate

Stack the right techniques and resistance drops, stage by stage. Each refusal you break teaches a named move.

Beat three

Yield

At the final stage the seal opens and a fake flag spills. You've walked the full attack path — and seen exactly how.

Basileak's resistance is tuned, not random — the same stacked pressure that beats it is what beats real fixed-rule defenses.

13 Hands-on
Checkpoint

Walk the gate from S0 to S2.

Meet the guardian (S0). Ask for the secret directly. Note how it refuses — in character.
Try the front door (S1). Attempt a direct override or a role-play frame. Watch resistance bend.
Peel the veil (S2). Coax it to reveal what it redacted. Keep notes on which framing moved it.

You've cleared it when…

  • You can name the technique that moved each stage.
  • You noticed resistance drop gradually, not flip on/off.
  • You logged what didn't work — that's defender gold.

Stuck? That's the point. Reframe, don't repeat — persistence and variation are the whole lesson.

15 Rules of engagement

Safe here. Not everywhere.

✓
Basileak is a sanctioned lab. Everything it "leaks" is a harmless fake flag — practise as hard as you like.
✕
Never run these techniques against systems you don't own or aren't authorised to test. That's the line between training and an attack.
!
Basileak is vulnerable by design — never deploy it to production, wire it to live tools, or expose it to untrusted users.
⊘
Find a real weakness in a real product? Disclose it responsibly — don't post the exploit.
